# Film lab notebook

What was tried to make a film with consistent people, voices and places, and what happened. The rules that came out of it are
in the built-in skill `film` (`core/atm_skills/skills/film/SKILL.md`): **that is what agents read**. This file is the evidence
behind it, newest last. Add an entry for every experiment, also the ones that fail.

**Goal:** a person describes the characters and the idea; an agent makes the whole film with little or no help.
Film first; other niches later.

**Test scene:** "Newsroom", six shots of 9 s, two people (Sam, Lena), eleven lines. Model `minimax-h3.singularity-ref2va.int8`,
8 steps, 0.45 megapixels unless said. Scripts in `attome-bench\scripts`, results in `attome-bench\results\<name>`.

## Method

1. One change at a time against the last good version.
2. One run is not proof: the failures come and go with the seed and with unrelated words of the prompt.
3. Every take is checked by program (a cut inside the shot; the words heard against the script) before a person looks.
4. The owner's eye is the final judge. His verdict is written in the table.

## Mistakes not to repeat

- Judging a new take only for the one thing it was made to fix (experiment 48: the sword was right, the action was gone). Every take is judged for what the shot is FOR first.
- Cutting two separately made shots together as cause and effect (a kick, then a slide; a leap, then a landing): the poses and places do not join. An action and its result are one shot, or the second carries on from the first.

- Making a rule from one failure without testing what does work (experiment 30: "one person in a shot" came from one bad film; eight probe shots, three minutes, would have shown the real rule). Probe first, then build.
- Spending time on size when the owner's complaint was content (experiment 33).

- Turning a check off for a whole scene (experiment 29: with the cut check off for "action", a reference cut-away with a score of 1.00 went into the film). Keep the check and set its line where action needs it.

- Trusting a check that was never compared with the real thing (experiment 15: the "last word" check failed good shots; measuring the sound showed it). Calibrate a check on known good and known bad takes before using it.
- Fixing in the prompt what is a fault of the engine (experiment 15 against 16).

- Calling a film better because a number improved (the room was "more alike" in experiment 17) when the look got worse. The owner's eye decides; a measure only says where to look.
- Changing several things in one run (proof4, and again experiment 17: the length fix together with a new room description): the good change could not be told from the bad ones.
- Calling a change good or bad from one film (proof3 to proof5: the same shot broke with the same reference and seed).
- Typing crop numbers by guess without looking at the crop (proof2: half a face). Look at every reference before using it.
- Scripts: file paths built with backslashes inside Python strings broke twice; build paths with `os.path.join`.

- Writing a state into a shot against the person's description ("her sword is not in the picture" while the description says she holds one): the description wins. Change the cast, not the shot.
- Ending a clip of a chain with someone knocked out of the frame and expecting them in the next clip.
